# ASA ruling: AHDB (Agriculture and Horticulture Development Board), May 2026
Outcome: Upheld in part (1 of 3 issues upheld: the carbon footprint claims in the two press ads; the other two issues were not upheld)
Medium: TV, national press, website (own site), social (Instagram), seen September 2024 to February 2025
Claim type: environmental (carbon footprint comparison; grazing imagery)
CAP clauses: Breached: 3.1, 3.3, 3.7, 11.3, 11.4 (press ads only). Investigated, no breach: CAP 11.1; BCAP 3.1, 3.2, 3.9, 9.2, 9.5 (TV ads). Assessed under the pre-7 April 2025 wording of the rules.
What the ad said: The "Let's Eat Balanced" campaign promoted British beef, lamb and dairy. Two press ads claimed British beef "has a carbon footprint that's half the global average" and British milk "a third lower than the global average", with a footnote saying "Full lifecycle emissions of CO2 eq per kg". TV ads, a website page and an Instagram post used grassy field imagery and referred to nutrition and "world-class" farming standards.
Why: The ASA found consumers would read "full lifecycle emissions" as including post-retail stages such as cooking and waste. AHDB's evidence only covered cradle-to-retail, so the claims were not substantiated, and a late consumer survey was inconclusive. The generic field imagery and the farming standards claims were not misleading. The imagery was a generic indication of provenance, not a claim about environmental benefit or that all cattle graze outdoors, and 87% of UK cattle farms use mixed housed and grazing systems.
